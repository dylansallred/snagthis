Which audio track is which? Name them, then let people listen.

Two independent choices: how a track is labelled and how a sample is heard. Both previews are live: open menus, press the pixel play button, use the keyboard. Rest the pointer in a section and press its number key to switch options. Sample audio is synthetic (tones and your browser's speech voices); the invented languages behind the cinejoy tracks are for demonstration only.

What cinejoy.pk actually sendsMaster playlist with four renditions: TYPE=AUDIO,GROUP-ID="audio",NAME="Track 1"…"Track 4", DEFAULT=YES on Track 1 only. No LANGUAGE, CHANNELS or CHARACTERISTICS. Each fMP4 init segment says language=und, AAC stereo 128 kb/s. The site's own player also only says “Track 1–4”.
So no label can be inferredThe four tracks are genuinely different audio (waveforms uncorrelated at the same timestamp), but nothing names them. Only listening tells them apart, so a sample is the fix here, and correct names are the fix everywhere else.
A sample is cheapOne audio rendition is about 17 KB/s. A 10-second sample from 25% in costs about 130 KB and 2–3 requests (audio_2.m3u8, init, one or two segments). The CDN answers with Access-Control-Allow-Origin: * and no Referer is needed.

Behaviour in every option

  • One sample at a time. Starting another track stops the first. Samples are 10 s (6 s here) from 25% of the way in, so they catch dialogue instead of an intro.
  • Stops cleanly on Esc (the first Esc stops the sound, the second closes the menu), on choosing a track, on closing the menu or popup, and when the popup is hidden.
  • States: loading (stepped pixel spinner, <1 s), playing (stop square, progress), failed (amber “Sample unavailable”; the track can still be chosen), one track (no Audio group; the trigger stays 1080p).
  • Keyboard (popup): ↑/↓ move, Enter chooses, Space or → plays/stops the focused track's sample. The sample button is a separate menuitem reached with → and labelled “Play a sample of Track 2”. Status is announced politely.
  • Reduced motion: no meter or spinner animation; progress moves in whole seconds.

Label rules (recommended)

  • Language from LANGUAGE via Intl.DisplayNames; ignore und, mul, zxx, qaa–qtz and codes it returns unchanged.
  • Keep NAME only when it adds something (“Commentary”, “Director's cut mix”); drop “Track 2”, “Audio”, “und”, or a copy of the code.
  • Tags: channels (1 Mono, 2 Stereo, 6 5.1, 8 7.1, …/JOC Atmos), AD from public.accessibility.describes-video, Commentary, and “Default” from DEFAULT=YES.
  • Collapse identical tracks repeated across GROUP-IDs; if two labels still match, add “· 2”.
  • Nothing known: “Track 2”, “Unknown language”, and a note to play a sample.

    

    

      
Implementation path

      
    
        
  • Chrome popup: reuse popup/source-preview.js' bounded fetch loader and Origin/Referer session, but load the audio rendition playlist directly into an <audio> with hls.js 1.5.20 (startPosition = 25%, maxBufferLength 10, stop after 10 s). Unmuted playback is allowed because the user clicked. Verified against cinejoy: 3 s played from 5:20 with 132 KB fetched.
    • 
        
  • Desktop: POST /v1/media/audio-sample in downloader-api runs bundled ffmpeg with the inspection's scoped headers: -ss 25% -i audio_N.m3u8 -t 10 -vn -c:a aac -b:a 64k → an 84 KB m4a in ~4.6 s; the renderer plays it from the local bridge. Faster: stream fMP4 segments through a bounded proxy and play in the renderer with hls.js.

  • Muxed-only sources (no separate audio rendition, DASH, MP4 with several audio streams): desktop ffmpeg -map 0:a:N; the popup shows labels only.
