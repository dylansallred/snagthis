Make desktop Settings easier to read.

Same sidebar sheet, same settings. These options change only the type, section headers, rows and rail. Option 1 is the sheet as it ships. Options 2 to 5 share the same copy rewrites, so you can compare the visual treatment on its own. Click an option or press 1–5. Change sections with the rail or the buttons above the window. Every control works.

What reads badly today measured from the running app at ?gallery&sheet=settings&section=…
  1. Two titles, 100px before contentThe 51px “Settings” bar (13.5px/600) sits above the section title (16px/620). The first setting starts about 135px down the sheet, and the bar crosses above the rail.
  2. Group headings are smaller than the labels under themIn Advanced and Updates, “Speed”, “File names” and “Online lookups” are 12.5px/600. The row labels under them are 13px/550 in the same colour, so the headings look like one more setting.
  3. Icon tiles compete with the text28px filled tiles appear on Downloads, App and Appearance, but not on Advanced, Updates or Chrome. The orange folder tile is the strongest colour in the sheet. The tiles push every label 40px to the right and repeat what the label already says.
  4. Helper text is too close to label textLabels are 13px and hints are 12px. Only weight and a 60% grey separate them. The Chrome pane sets status, steps, notes and the fallback in the same 12px grey at a 1.6–2 line height, so nothing stands out.
  5. Controls sit in three different placesFolder buttons sit on the left under the path. Selects are 132px on the right. API key fields run the full width with the eye button outside. Update buttons sit on the left. Your eye has to jump between three columns.
  6. Some actions look like helper text“Report a site…”, “Clear temporary download data” and “Restore default speed and file names” are 12px grey text with no button styling. When disabled, the last one becomes a stray sentence (“Speed and file names use the defaults”).
  7. Status lines have no labels“You’re up to date.” sits between a switch and a button with nothing to say what it describes. “Chrome is connected…” is a paragraph, not a status. The version appears in both the rail footer and Updates.
  8. Chrome pane shows setup when it is doneThe pairing steps still show after Chrome is connected. “last seen just now” wraps onto its own line, and the fallback sentence wraps so “instead.” sits alone.
  9. Short sections leave a lot of empty spaceDownloads, App and Appearance end 35–55% of the way down the pane. Nothing sits at the bottom. “Changes save automatically” exists only for screen readers.
  10. Paths look like helper textThe folder shows in 12px grey, the same as a hint, and is cut off after two lines. The default reads “Downloads/SnagThis (default)”, which looks like an explanation, not a value.
  11. Steppers put the range first“1–16 · more downloads share your connection” leads with the range. People read the numbers before the reason.
  12. The rail looks heavier than the contentThe rail and cards share the 8% surface, but the pane is 6.5%. Rail labels are 12.5px and content is 13px. The selected tab’s 2px bar is the only use of the accent.
